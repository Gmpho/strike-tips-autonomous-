# Value Betting & the NHRA Merit Rating System

## The Merit Rating (MR) System

Introduced in **1999** by the National Horseracing Authority (NHRA), the MR system standardises handicapping in SA racing.

**Core principle:** 1 MR point ≈ 0.5 kg in weight carried.

- Ratings typically range from 0 to 140+
- Top-class horses often exceed MR 100
- Current top-rated (as of Jun 2026): Buffalo Storm Cody (MR 132), See It Again (MR 130), Questioning (MR 129)

## How MR Changes After a Race

The NHRA handicapper recalculates MR after each race:

1. A "line horse" is selected (usually the runner-up or a consistent performer)
2. Beaten distances are converted to MR points (~1 length ≈ 2 MR points)
3. Weight-for-age (WFA) differentials are factored in
4. Quality of opposition is considered

This creates a **self-correcting system** — horses that win too easily get raised, horses that run well against better company from a low draw may be underrated.

## Finding Value

### 1. Market vs MR Comparison

Compare the market odds with what MR differentials suggest:

- A horse with MR 90 dropping to MR 84 (dropping 6 points in 3 runs) may be "well in" at the weights if the drop is due to form slump rather than genuine decline
- Conversely, a horse raised 8 points for a single win may be "vulnerable" — required to carry more weight against better horses

### 2. Weight-for-Age (WFA) Angles

SA uses WFA scales to account for developmental differences, especially for younger horses:

- A 3yo receiving 8.5kg from an older horse over 1400m early in the season represents a significant advantage
- As the season progresses, WFA differentials narrow
- A 3yo that beat older horses "at the weights" early-season is likely well-above-average

### 3. Track Specialists

Some horses perform significantly better at specific tracks:
- Durbanville produces course specialists
- Horses with proven form at a track can be trusted more than those without
- A horse switching from Highveld to Cape Town (or vice versa) faces altitude and surface adjustment

### 4. Class Drop/Distance Switch

- **Class drop:** A horse that ran in Grade 1 company now dropped to a MR 80 Handicap may be significantly classier than its rivals
- **Distance switch:** A horse with stamina breeding stepping up to 2000m for the first time may outperform MR expectations
- **Layoff:** Horses returning from a 60+ day layoff typically need the run — unless they have a history of fresh form

### 5. Draw Analysis

Each track has distinct draw biases (see individual track profiles):
- Kenilworth: Low draws favoured in sprints
- Durbanville: Low draws marked advantage
- Fairview: High draws favoured on straight course
- Turffontein: Low barriers favoured at 1400m/1600m
- Vaal: Low draws favoured at 1200m/1450m (Classic), high draws at 1000m
- Greyville: Inside draws favoured up to 1900m
- Scottsville: High numbers favoured on straight in soft going

### 6. Going Preference

Check each runner's record on today's going:
- A horse with 3 wins from 4 starts on Soft going running on a Soft track is overpriced if the market ignores the stat
- A horse with 0 from 8 on Heavy going is a lay if the track is Heavy

## The Value Calculation

True Value = (Estimated True Probability × Decimal Odds) > 1.0

Example:
- You estimate a horse has a 25% true chance
- Market offers 5.0 (4/1)
- EV = 0.25 × 5.0 = 1.25 (25% edge)
- This is value

Where your estimate comes from:
- MR differentials + recent form + track bias + going + class + draw

## Key Difference from International Racing

SA's MR system creates tighter handicaps than most international systems. MR Handicap races have the 4th finisher within 1 length of the winner ~7% of the time (vs 3% for non-MR races) and within 5 lengths ~90% of the time (vs 66%).

This means:
- Smaller edges (MR compresses the field)
- Need to bet selectively
- Small number of high-conviction bets beats many marginal bets
